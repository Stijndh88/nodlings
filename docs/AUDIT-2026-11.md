# Foundations audit, 14 November 2026

Stijn's question: did we start off wrong? Is what we built useful, is there enough reason for a Nodling to
try things with wood, and could Nodlings invent something and change their own behaviour without us coding it?

Short answer: **the research tooling is good and worth keeping. The world and the creature are not a
foundation that open-ended invention can grow on, and the live world itself is broken.** This document
gives the measurements, the verdict per part, and a concrete redesign ("World 2").

Everything here was measured on the live world at day 2063 (`loop/world.json.gz`, commit `220710a`) or
on the fixed exam with 100 living genomes and 8 test worlds. "t" is the paired t statistic over those 8
worlds; above 1.9 means the difference is real at the usual 5% level.

## 1. What the measurements say

### 1.1 The live world has silted up and nobody can reach water

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| Land cells that are impassable walls (a stack of 3 or more) | **50%** (7,412 of 14,712) |
| Of those walls, the share that contains fiber | 92% |
| Shore cells that are still open | 13% (129 of 971) |
| Living Nodlings that can walk to water | **0 of 500** |
| Drinks in 3,000 ticks (1.5 million Nodling-ticks) | **0** |
| Ticks any Nodling spent next to water | 0 |

How it happened: eating a plant drops a seed (60%) or a fiber (15%) on a nearby cell, up to a height of 3.
A stack of 3 is a wall. A stack that contains fiber cannot be knocked down by a Nodling and never weathers,
and a Nodling cannot pick anything off a cell it cannot enter. So walls are made by eating, and they are
permanent. After 2,000 days the population lives in sealed pockets.

Consequences:
- **"Thirst" is not thirst.** Every Nodling dies at age 2,223 at the latest, because that is how long the
  water it is born with lasts. The roadmap read the 30% thirst deaths as "a third never drinks". In fact none
  can. Thirst is a lifespan limit.
- **The thirst experiments cannot work in this world.** Raising `ENV.thirst` only shortens every life. That is
  what `era-thirst-3x` did.
- **The "village" is silt.** The 2,884-cell cluster and the 7,121 bricks are mostly the product of walls that
  form on their own, clay that seeps out next to bricks, and clay that turns into brick next to brick.
- In a fresh exam world, where water is everywhere, the evolved Nodlings do drink by accident (they press
  "interact" 60% of the time), but when water runs out in 440 ticks, 87 of 100 still die of thirst. There is no
  drinking behaviour in the gene pool.

Run `node loop/audit-world.js` to reproduce the first table.

### 1.2 What a Nodling "feels" is almost entirely our bonuses

Per decision, the learning signal is made of three parts. Measured averages:

| Part of the reward | Average size | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Real change in the body (energy, water, temperature) | 0.022 | 11% |
| Curiosity term | 0.043 | 21% |
| Hand-written bonuses | 0.140 | 68% |

The reward is positive on 97.8% of all decisions. The largest bonuses are "stand near bricks" (84,000 times
in 3,000 ticks), "hear a call while a predator is near" (61,000) and "cool down" (37,000).

**The building bonus is being farmed.** 95% of rewarded "builds" (1,135 of 1,197 in 2,000 ticks) are a
Nodling picking a brick up and putting it back on the same cell in the same tick. Real hauling (pick up, carry
8 cells, build) happened 5 times in 3,000 ticks. Wood was picked up and carried off 74 times by 500 Nodlings in 3,000 ticks.

### 1.3 Nothing they do with materials helps them

Exam score with one faculty switched off. "Worth" is how much the score drops without it.

| Faculty switched off | Score | Worth | t |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nothing (normal) | 2,317 | | |
| Random brains for comparison | 321 | | |
| Hands: grab, drop and strike | 2,320 | 0% | -0.05 |
| All senses about materials, structures and fire | 2,482 | -7% | -1.6 |
| All bonuses | 2,388 | -3% | -0.8 |
| Bonuses and curiosity (body-only reward) | 2,459 | -6% | -1.7 |
| Learning during life | 2,416 | -4% | -1.2 |
| Memory | 2,225 | +4% | 1.9 |
| Senses about the nearest Nodling | 2,014 | **+13%** | 3.1 |
| Hearing | 2,343 | -1% | -0.4 |
| **Calling** | 2,886 | **-25%** | **-5.8** |

Read this as: a Nodling with no hands, blind to every material, scores the same. All the machinery for wood,
stone, planks, clay, brick, kilns, bridges and hunting adds nothing to survival or offspring. Calling is a
pure cost: Nodlings call on 59% of all ticks because calling is paid by bonuses, and it costs them a quarter
of their score. What evolution really taught them is: walk to plants, eat, stay near other Nodlings.

### 1.4 A Nodling cannot see most of the things it is paid to use

The eyes report the nearest flora, water, wood, stone, data, structure and fire. They do **not** report clay,
brick, plank, meat, cooked meat, seed, fiber or critters. A Nodling cannot see the brick it is rewarded for
picking up, the clay it is rewarded for carrying to a fire, or the animal it is rewarded for hunting. It also
cannot see what lies on the cell under it, only how high the stack is.

### 1.5 The physics is a short list of recipes

`world.js` says "property bags, never a recipe table", but the rules are written per material name:

- strike wood: 25% chance it becomes a plank
- clay next to fire becomes brick; meat next to fire becomes cooked meat
- special rules for brick only: seeds next to brick sprout twice as fast, meat next to brick keeps twice as
  long, clay appears next to brick, clay next to brick fires itself, nothing above brick burns
- a predator cannot catch a Nodling that has 2 durable neighbours (a rule, not a wall that blocks)
- lightning prefers to strike next to clay

What a held object does: nothing. Carrying a stone does not make a strike stronger. Nodlings cannot make
fire, only lightning can. The `weight` property is never used. So the space of things to discover is exactly
the list we typed in. There is nothing to invent, because nothing combines.

Does wood have real uses? A few: stacks insulate (one wood stack beside you saves about 0.1 energy per tick
on a cold winter night, twice the resting metabolism), wood floats and makes bridges, walls block movement.
But nobody has ever died of cold (0 temperature deaths in the whole recorded history), a year lasts 30 Nodling
lifetimes so no Nodling sees two winters, and one fake build pays the maximum reward a brain can receive, far
more than the physics ever pays for a real one.

### 1.6 Lives are too short to learn anything

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Average age at death | 1,630 ticks, about one in-world day (a day is 1,600 ticks) |
| Decisions in a life (the brain thinks every other tick) | about 800 |
| Meals in a life | median 18 |
| Lifetimes per in-world year | about 30 |

Learning needs repetition: try, see what happens, try again. With 18 meals and one day of life, there is no
second winter, no second anything. Genes will always beat learning in a world like that. A Nodling also
starts life alone with an empty brain state; there is no childhood next to a parent and nothing is passed on
except genes. Culture has no channel.

### 1.7 The strongest selection in the world is for being different

Who breeds is decided by a lottery (roadmap finding), multiplied by two hand-made factors:

- kin crowding: breeding chance times 0.40 to 0.95 depending on how similar the neighbours are in colour,
  diet **and brain size**
- novelty: times 1.13 to 1.36 for behaving unlike past breeders

Meanwhile offspring count correlates only 0.21 with meals eaten. Having a brain of a different size from your
neighbours is rewarded directly. That is a likely cause of the brain size wandering from 205 to 77 to 114
connections with no reason, and of the skill score swinging 40% between cycles.

### 1.8 The brain is not the bottleneck

114 connections, 37 hidden neurons (29 of them memory cells), 99% of connections plastic. The network type
(NEAT), mutation and crossover are fine. The learning rule is weak (no memory of which action earned a delayed
reward), but a better rule cannot help while 89% of the signal is bonus and curiosity and there is nothing to
learn.

## 2. Verdict per part

| Part | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Headless runner, fixed exam, switched-off-faculty tests, replicated A/B, experiments on GitHub Actions, CI, determinism | **Keep.** This is the valuable part. It is what made this audit possible. |
| Brain core (`brain.js`: NEAT genome, mutation, crossover, memory cells) | **Keep**, extend the learning rule. |
| Terrain, seasons, day and night, flora growth, critters, predators with brains | **Keep the ideas**, retune time scales. |
| Renderer, observer, dashboard | **Keep**, adapt. |
| The 45 reward bonuses, curiosity in the reward | **Remove all at once.** They are worth 0 to -6% and they are 89% of the signal. |
| Sound bonuses | **Remove.** Calling costs 25% of the score. |
| Brick special rules, clay seep, kiln lightning, nest head start, predator shelter rule, fiber binding, `data` | **Remove.** Scripted outcomes. Fiber binding broke the world. |
| Kin crowding with brain size, novelty breeding bonus, hall-of-fame top-up | **Remove.** They select for difference, not for skill. |
| Senses by material name | **Replace** with senses by property. |
| Per-material recipes | **Replace** with a few rules over properties. |
| Time scales (life = 1 day, year = 30 lives) | **Replace.** A life must span several years. |
| The live world file (4,349 generations) | **Archive and restart.** It is sealed, its gene pool has no drinking, and new senses make old genomes unusable anyway. |

So yes: for the goal of invention, we started off wrong in three places. We paid for behaviours instead of
making them useful, we wrote recipes instead of physics, and we gave Nodlings lives too short and too
solitary to learn or teach. None of that is fixed by more cycles of the current loop.

## 3. What is realistic to expect

Plain expectations, so the goal is honest:

- **Consciousness: no.** Nothing here approaches it and no known method does.
- **"Rewriting their own behaviour": yes, in a concrete sense.** A brain whose connections change during
  life, that decides itself when to learn, and that can copy what a successful neighbour does, does change
  its own behaviour within a lifetime. That is buildable (section 4.4).
- **Invention: yes, small ones.** With brains of about 100 connections, expect discoveries like "strike with
  a stone in hand and the animal dies in one blow", "food next to the fire is worth more", "drop wood on a
  dying fire and it keeps burning", "this year the red plant is the poisonous one". Not agriculture, not
  architecture.
- **Culture: possible in a simple form.** A habit that one Nodling learned and that outlives it because the
  young copy it. This can be measured with a test where young Nodlings grow up with or without experienced adults.
- It takes time. Evolution here is slow and noisy. Think weeks of loop time per step, and a result that shows
  up in a measured number before it shows up on screen.

## 4. The redesign: World 2

Six principles. Each one answers a finding above.

1. **One small rulebook over properties, no material names in the rules.** (1.5)
2. **Nodlings see properties, not names.** (1.4)
3. **Only the body rewards.** No bonuses, ever. (1.2, 1.3)
4. **Lives long enough to learn, with a childhood beside adults.** (1.6)
5. **Nothing piles up forever.** Everything rots, burns, weathers or gets eaten. (1.1)
6. **Invention is counted, never paid.** (1.2)

### 4.1 Physics: properties and five rules

Every material is a row of numbers. Proposed starting table (values are a first guess, to be tuned):

| Material | mass | hard | fuel | food | insul | floats | sharp | rots | when heated | when broken |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| wood | 2 | 2 | 40 | 0 | 0.6 | yes | 0 | slow | | 2 sticks |
| stick | 1 | 1 | 15 | 0 | 0.2 | yes | 0 | medium | | |
| stone | 5 | 5 | 0 | 0 | 0.9 | no | 0 | never | | flake |
| flake | 1 | 5 | 0 | 0 | 0 | no | 1 | never | | |
| clay | 3 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0.5 | no | 0 | never | brick | |
| brick | 4 | 4 | 0 | 0 | 1.0 | no | 0 | never | | |
| plant A, plant B | 0.5 | 0 | 5 | varies | 0.1 | yes | 0 | medium | | seed |
| seed | 0.1 | 0 | 2 | 4 | 0 | yes | 0 | medium | | |
| meat | 1 | 0 | 0 | 70 | 0 | no | 0 | fast | cooked meat | |
| cooked meat | 1 | 0 | 0 | 110 | 0 | no | 0 | medium | | |

The rules never mention a material. They read the numbers:

- **Carry.** One item. Moving costs extra energy in proportion to its mass.
- **Place.** Dropped on the cell ahead or underfoot. An item that floats makes a water cell walkable. A stack
  of 3 blocks movement for everyone, predators included (this replaces the predator shelter rule).
- **Strike.** Force = 1 + mass x hardness of what is in the hand. If the force beats the hardness of the
  target, the target breaks into its "when broken" parts, or loses its top item, or (an animal) dies. A sharp
  item in the hand doubles the force against things with hardness 0 to 2. Striking a hard target with a hard
  item makes a spark with some chance; a spark lights anything with fuel on that cell.
- **Heat.** A burning item heats its neighbours and burns for as many ticks as it has fuel. Fuel dropped
  beside a fire catches. A heated item turns into its "when heated" form. Heat warms bodies and burns them when too close.
- **Decay.** Every item with a "rots" value disappears after a while. Stacks weather unless supported by neighbours.
  Nothing binds a stack forever.

Why this is different from today: with 10 materials, 3 places an item can be (hand, ahead, underfoot) and 4
verbs, there are several hundred possible combinations, and the outcome of each follows from the numbers.
We do not list the useful ones. Adding a material later is one new row, and every rule and every sense
works with it at once.

Two things genes cannot know, so learning has a job:
- Each year the world draws which of plant A and plant B is nourishing (food 42) and which is slightly
  poisonous (food -10). They look different (see senses) but which is which changes.
- Later: where the good patches are, which moves each year.

### 4.2 Senses: properties of three places, plus directions by need

- **Body:** energy, water, temperature, pain (recent damage), age, mass carried.
- **Three places** (in hand, cell ahead, cell underfoot): the property row of the top item there: hard, fuel,
  food-smell (a fixed look per plant kind, not its true food value), heat, mass, sharp, plus stack height.
  That is about 21 senses and it makes every present and future material visible.
- **Directions** to the nearest: water, heat, edible-looking thing, hard thing, fuel, wall, animal, Nodling, predator.
- **Others:** what the nearest Nodling is holding (property row), its last action, whether its body state just
  improved or got worse, whether it is an adult or a child, kinship. Sound in and out, with no bonus attached.
- **Memory:** 4 memory outputs fed back as senses.

### 4.3 Body, time and selection

- **Reward** = change in body state only: energy, water, temperature comfort, pain. The weight of each of the
  four drives is a gene, so evolution decides what feels good. No curiosity term, no bonuses.
- **Time.** Day 400 ticks, year 8 days = 3,200 ticks. Adult at 400 ticks, ageing from 6,400, natural life
  about 3 years. The brain thinks every tick. To pay for longer lives the population limit drops to about 200.
- **Childhood.** A newborn follows nobody by rule, but it starts beside its parent and sees less far, and
  while it is a child it can be fed (a Nodling can drop food on the cell ahead; nothing rewards it, kin
  selection has to find it).
- **Selection.** No hard cap lottery: food sets the carrying capacity and crowding costs energy (the roadmap's
  `soft-cap`). Breeding chance rises with energy surplus (`breed-by-surplus`). No kin or novelty multipliers.
  Winters are cold enough to kill an unsheltered, unfed Nodling some years, and every Nodling lives through several.
- **Water** must be reachable: the decay rule and a CI check (`canReachWater` in `loop/audit-world.js` must stay above 0.9) guard it.

### 4.4 Changing their own behaviour: three layers

1. **A learning rule that can assign credit.** Each plastic connection keeps a short fading memory of its
   recent activity (an eligibility trace). Reward minus its running average is applied to that memory. This is
   the roadmap's `reward-baseline` plus `eligibility-trace`.
2. **The brain decides when to learn.** One new output, `learn`, multiplies the learning signal. A brain can
   evolve to switch learning on after a surprise and off when things go well. This is the honest version of
   "a brain that rewrites itself": its own activity gates its own rewiring.
3. **Copying.** A gene `imitate` (0 to 1), strongest in childhood. While it is on, plastic connections are
   nudged so that the Nodling's own action moves toward the last action of the nearest adult whose body state
   just improved. A habit that one Nodling learned can then spread to others and outlive it. That is culture
   in its smallest form, and whether it evolves is up to selection (the gene can go to 0).

### 4.5 Measuring invention without paying for it

- **Technique ledger.** Every outcome of a verb is logged as a type: (verb, class of item in hand, class of
  target, result), for example (strike, hard+sharp, animal, kill) or (drop, fuel, burning cell, fire extended).
  Reported per cycle: the number of distinct types performed by at least 5% of adults, and the day each type
  was first seen. No reward is attached. This is the "did they invent something" number.
- **Exam, version 2.** Same idea as today, new tasks: `base`, `scarce`, `thirst`, `winter` (a full year),
  `relearn` (the good plant flips halfway). Switched-off tests: `handsGain`, `learningGain`, `memoryGain`,
  `socialGain`, and `cultureGain` (children raised with experienced adults against children raised alone).
- **World health.** `loop/audit-world.js` every cycle: wall share, open shore, reach of water.

### 4.6 Code layout

Today `Nodling.tick` is one 300-line function with physics, bonuses and bookkeeping mixed. World 2 splits by job:

| File | Job |
|---|---|
| `src/materials.js` | The property table. Data only. |
| `src/physics.js` | The five rules. Pure functions of (world, cell, item). No Nodling code. |
| `src/world.js` | Grid, terrain, climate, flora, decay. |
| `src/body.js` | Needs, metabolism, temperature, death, breeding. |
| `src/senses.js` | The sense vector. One documented table of indices. |
| `src/actions.js` | Brain outputs to physics calls. Emits ledger events. |
| `src/brain.js` | Genome, network, learning rule. |
| `src/sim.js`, `src/metrics.js` | Tick loop, exam, ledger. |

Each physics rule gets a unit test. A "no Nodlings for 20,000 ticks" test checks that the world does not silt
up or burn down on its own.

## 5. How to get there

Stages S1 to S3 are a rebuild and should be done by a strong model in one go on a branch, because they
change everything at once and cannot be A/B tested against the old world. From S4 on, the normal loop can
carry it with the existing experiment tooling.

| Stage | What | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| S0 | Tag the current state `v1-final`, move the old world to `archive/`, set the loop to batch-only | Tag exists, loop submits no experiments |
| S1 | `materials.js`, `physics.js`, `world.js` with decay; unit tests; empty-world stability test | Tests green; an empty world keeps wall share under 5% and open shore above 90% for 20,000 ticks |
| S2 | `body.js`, `senses.js`, `actions.js`, learning rule layers 1 and 2, selection without lottery | A fresh world from random brains holds a population for 100,000 ticks; CI determinism green |
| S3 | Exam version 2, technique ledger, dashboard; baseline with `--aa` noise floor | Evolved brains beat random on `base` after 200,000 ticks |
| S4 | Loop resumes on the new roadmap phases (selection, learning pays, copying, tools) | See ROADMAP |

The old world stays viewable under its tag. Nothing is deleted.

## 6. How this was measured

Scripts ran against `loop/world.json.gz` at day 2063.41 in a copy of the sim with counters inserted (no
change to the repository's sim files). Exam runs: the 100 genomes the probes would sample, seeds 101 to 808,
3,000 ticks, task `base`, one faculty removed per run. `loop/audit-world.js` reproduces section 1.1 and the
brain numbers; the other counters were one-off instrumentation and are described precisely enough above to rebuild.
